Proof
Show diagonal-split proof
Turn this on to split the quadrilateral into two triangles along a diagonal — exactly like SOME in the textbook. Each triangle's angles sum to 180°, and together they make the quadrilateral's four angles, so 180° + 180° = 360°.
Why it's always 360°
Any simple quadrilateral can be split by one diagonal into two triangles. Each triangle's interior angles sum to 180° (a fixed fact). Adding both triangles counts every corner of the quadrilateral exactly once, so the total is 180° + 180° = 360° — no matter how the vertices are dragged, as long as the sides don't cross.
